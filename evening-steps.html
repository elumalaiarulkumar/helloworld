A short, practical guide

Simple evening steps that make rest more likely

Rest isn't just something that happens when you lie down — it's something your evening either sets up or works against. These steps are small on purpose, so they're easy to actually keep.

2–3 hours before bed

Give your body a cutoff point

Your body needs a clear signal that the day is ending, not a gradual fade.

  • Have your last big meal and any caffeine well before this window — both can keep your system running when it should be slowing down.
  • If you exercise, do the hard version earlier in the day. A short walk in the evening is fine; a workout that spikes your heart rate close to bed usually isn't.
1 hour before bed

Close the day on purpose

Most racing thoughts at bedtime are unfinished business from the day looking for somewhere to land.

Write down tomorrow's first three tasks, and anything still open from today. It doesn't need to be organized — the point is getting it out of your head and onto paper so your mind can stop holding it.

Then switch off work mode deliberately — close the laptop, put your phone in another room or on do-not-disturb, and treat this as the actual end of the workday, even if more could technically be done.

30–45 minutes before bed

Dim everything, including yourself

Bright light and screens tell your brain it's still daytime. Low light tells it otherwise.

  • Lower the lights in your space, or switch to a lamp instead of overhead lighting.
  • If you're on a screen, put it on night mode, or better, swap it for something analog — a book, stretching, folding laundry, anything that doesn't ask much of your eyes or your attention.
In bed

Let your body downshift last

This is where you stop trying to solve anything and just let your system slow down.

A few slow breaths — in for 4, out for 6 — or a slow scan down your body noticing where you're holding tension, from your jaw down to your feet, releasing what you can.

If your mind is still busy, that's normal. You're not trying to force sleep — you're just giving your body permission to stop being on duty.

If you only do one thing

Pick the task-dump step. Getting tomorrow's first three things out of your head and onto paper handles more racing-thoughts insomnia than almost anything else on this list.

If sleep stays hard most nights for a few weeks despite steps like these, it's worth mentioning to a doctor — ongoing sleep trouble is worth looking into directly rather than working around indefinitely.
